About

Sadaf Zehra is currently working as an Instructor of Computer Science at FAST - National University of Computer and Emerging Sciences (FAST-NUCES), Karachi, and as Visiting Faculty at Bahria University. With a passion for few-shot learning and language models, she completed an MS in Data Science from NED University of Engineering and Technology with a CGPA of 3.81/4.00, where she received a merit-based scholarship. Her MS thesis, supervised by Dr. Muhammad Umer Farooq, built a conditional variational autoencoder that writes characters in a new handwriting style after seeing only five examples. She has co-authored a paper published by IEEE at FET'26, which she presented herself, and her recent research connects code language models with cognitive science to build AI assistants that adapt to how each programmer reads and understands code.

Alongside her studies, she has worked as a Computer Vision Intern at The Disrupt Labs, where she built real-time smoke and package detection models for factory cameras, and as a ServiceNow Developer at Faralone Enterprises and Consultant. She has taught at the University of Karachi and the Aawaz Institute of Media & Management Sciences, and coached intermediate students for nearly three years. At FAST-NUCES she writes her own lab manuals, supervises student projects and serves on Final Year Project evaluation juries. Teaching programming every day, and seeing how differently students read the same code, is what led her to study program comprehension. These experiences have strengthened her teaching, communication and research skills, as well as her ability to explain complex ideas to different audiences.

Her research interests include few-shot learning and user modelling, language models for program comprehension, computational cognitive modelling, interpretable and faithful machine learning, vision-language and multimodal learning, and deep generative models. Beyond her academic and professional pursuits, she is an ardent writer and painter with a keen eye for aesthetics. She enjoys exploring topics related to psychology, comparative religion and theology, and is always eager to seize new opportunities and embrace fresh challenges with enthusiasm and curiosity.

Language Models and Program Comprehension

  • Project Title: GLANCE: Gaze-Driven Learner Profiling for Adaptive Pragmatic Code Explanations (2026)

    Description:
    Aligned the eye-tracking fixations of 154 programmers reading Java code (EMIP dataset) to code tokens and tested whether surprisal from code language models (Qwen2.5-Coder 0.5B and 1.5B) predicts how long different groups of readers look at each token, using held-out log-likelihood per participant. Profiled unseen developers from only 1, 3 or 5 short gaze windows with few-shot prototypes and a learned NCA metric, and explained each decision with coefficients, permutation importance and decision rules. The profile then drives a Rational Speech Acts speaker that chooses among LLM code explanations by trading informativity against a gaze-fitted reading cost, with retrieval grounding and checks for hallucinated identifiers.
    Tools: Python, PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers, Qwen2.5-Coder, EMIP Eye-Tracking Data, scikit-learn, Rational Speech Acts
    Code: GitHub
  • Project Title: STALE: Stale-Value Tracing in Artificial and Learner Environments (2026)

    Description:
    Built a benchmark of 400 generated Python programs that vary the number of variables (2 to 5), how often the queried variable is re-assigned (0 to 4) and the kind of re-assignment (augmented, swap, loop), with answers verified by execution and every error labelled as a stale value, another variable's value, or other. Tested code LLMs with 0, 2 and 4 demonstrations (exact McNemar tests) and located where variable values are stored inside the model with layer-wise probes and activation patching. Modelled tracing errors with an ACT-R style working-memory model, estimated a learner's memory capacity from a few answers with a Bayesian posterior, and compared personalised and fixed hint policies in simulation.
    Tools: Python, PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers, Qwen2.5-Coder, Linear Probes, Activation Patching, ACT-R
    Code: GitHub
  • Project Title: TAILOR: Taste-Aware Interpretable Learning of Reader Preferences (2026)

    Description:
    Generated 192 code explanations (24 Python functions in a 2×2×2 design of detail, example and confidence) and described each with nine interpretable features, including readability measured as surprisal under a reader language model. Trained a sparse mixture-of-experts reward model (L1 Bradley-Terry experts fitted with EM) on simulated developer preferences, and measured how well it recovers hidden developer types, how quickly it adapts to a new developer from 1 to 16 comparisons, and how faithful contribution-contrast explanations are compared with routing weights.
    Tools: Python, PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers, Qwen2.5-Coder, scikit-learn, Mixture of Experts, EM
    Code: GitHub
  • Project Title: Beyond English Identifiers: Modelling the Second-Language Cost of Program Comprehension (2026)

    Description:
    Renamed the identifiers of 20 Python functions in four styles (English, abbreviated, Roman Urdu, neutral), verified all 80 variants by execution, and scored how familiar each identifier word is to an English-centred and a multilingual language model (r = 0.87). Generated code explanations in English, Roman Urdu and Urdu script and checked them with back-translation: the model wrote Roman Urdu in only 1 of 16 cases, a gap for a language variety that millions of programming students use. On simulated classroom data, a short vocabulary test plus a few quiz answers chose the best explanation language for 86% of students.
    Tools: Python, PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers, Qwen2.5, SmolLM2, scikit-learn, Bayesian Inference
    Code: GitHub

Few-Shot and Generative Learning

  • Project Title: MS Thesis: Few-Shot Font Generation Using Conditional Variational Autoencoder (Aug 2025 - Jun 2026)
    Supervisor: Dr. Muhammad Umer Farooq, Department of Computer Science and Information Technology, NED University of Engineering and Technology

    Description:
    Built a conditional VAE that writes characters in a new handwriting style after seeing only five example images. It is trained with 5-way 5-shot episodes on Omniglot, so it works on alphabets it never saw in training, using a combined BCE + MAE + SSIM loss with KL annealing. Achieved SSIM 0.782 and FID 12.21, compared with the published results of eight few-shot font generation methods (FUNIT, MX-Font, DG-Font, CF-Font, NTF-Loc, VQ-Font, Diff-Font, SGD-Font).
    Tools: Python, TensorFlow/Keras, Conditional VAE, Few-Shot Learning, SSIM, FID
    Code: GitHub
  • Project Title: LangFont: Language-Guided Few-Shot Font Generation with Diffusion (2026)

    Description:
    Built a font generator that takes 1 to 10 example letters of a new font plus a short text such as "bold, rounded, handwritten" and draws any missing character. It fine-tunes Stable Diffusion 1.5 with LoRA and adds a style encoder (CLIP + small CNN) that turns the example letters into extra tokens next to the text. Built a dataset of 300 Google Fonts (62 characters each) with style descriptions generated by OpenCV shape analysis, and tested on 38 fonts from 30 unseen font families. LangFont cut FID from 184.6 to 54.1 (KID×1000 from 94.3 to 7.8) against a thesis-style CVAE baseline with 5 references, with higher CLIP image similarity (0.877 vs 0.776), while the CVAE kept a higher SSIM (0.818 vs 0.734). Ablations showed the reference images carry most of the style (text-only FID 73.7 vs 54.1) and quality stays stable from 1 to 10 references.
    Tools: Python, PyTorch, Stable Diffusion 1.5, LoRA (PEFT), CLIP, OpenCV, FID/KID
    Code: GitHub
  • Project Title: Beyond the Mean Prototype: Multi-Modal Class Representations for Few-Shot Learning (2026)

    Description:
    Improved Prototypical Networks by giving each class several prototypes (found with soft k-means) instead of one average, so classes with more than one style are described better. Tested on 600 tasks per setting (5/20-way, 1/5-shot) on normal and mixed-style Omniglot. Beat Prototypical Networks in all four 5-shot settings, with every gain confirmed by 95% confidence intervals (best: 92.24% vs 91.77% on mixed-style 20-way 5-shot), and matched it in 1-shot as expected.
    Tools: Python, PyTorch, Prototypical Networks, Soft k-Means, Meta-Learning, Omniglot
    Code: GitHub
  • Project Title: SCACM: Semantic Clustered Attention Conditioning for Few-Shot Generation on Unseen Alphabets (2026)

    Description:
    Built a controlled test on unseen Omniglot alphabets comparing three ways to condition a few-shot conditional VAE: averaging, attention and clustered attention (SCACM). Clustered attention matched but did not beat averaging (IWAE NLL 105.68 vs 105.58 nats), which I traced to the low variation inside Omniglot classes.
    Tools: Python, PyTorch, Conditional VAE, Attention, IWAE, Omniglot
    Code: GitHub
  • Project Title: Safe Over Short: Learning to Trace Unseen Characters with Reinforcement Learning (2026)

    Description:
    Turned handwriting tracing on Omniglot into a reinforcement learning task where the pen slips 5% of the time (1,852 training tasks and 500 test tasks from unseen alphabets), and implemented ten RL methods from scratch: dynamic programming, two Monte Carlo methods, Sarsa, Q-learning, Dyna-Q, DQN, REINFORCE, A2C and PPO. Showed that planning for slips beats the shortest path on unseen alphabets (95.0% vs 75.4% success); Sarsa, Q-learning and Dyna-Q reached about 93% success, close to the optimal 94.1%. Found the deep RL agents cheating the reward (ending episodes early) and fixed it with potential-based reward shaping.
    Tools: Python, PyTorch, NumPy, Dynamic Programming, Q-Learning, DQN, PPO, Reward Shaping
    Code: GitHub
  • Project Title: Controllable Text-to-Image Generation with Stable Diffusion and CLIP-Based Evaluation (2025)

    Description:
    Built a Stable Diffusion pipeline and measured prompt fidelity with CLIP score across sampling steps, guidance scale, image size and negative prompts. The score levelled off after 25 steps, peaked at guidance 7.5, and dropped for portrait images (28.62 to 25.66) and with negative prompts (28.51 to 27.26).
    Tools: Python, PyTorch, Hugging Face Diffusers, Stable Diffusion, CLIP
    Code: GitHub

Vision-Language, Computer Vision and Multimodal Learning

  • Project Title: Seeing with Words: Language-Guided Prototypes for One-Shot Recognition (2026)

    Description:
    Combined CLIP image prototypes with class text descriptions (CUB bird attributes and WordNet definitions) and compared eleven methods, including zero-shot text, image-text fusion, linear probes and a learned shot-aware gate, on 600 fixed 5-way episodes with 95% confidence intervals. Adding text raised 1-shot accuracy from 81.7% to 96.1% on CUB-200 (+14.3 points) and from 88.3% to 99.0% on mini-ImageNet (+10.7), while the gain shrank to about +2 points at 5-shot; all gains were significant on paired episodes. The learned gate shifted weight to text when examples were scarce (83% at 1-shot vs 60% at 5-shot on CUB), but simple validation-tuned fusion stayed best, and long descriptions gave no gain over class names.
    Tools: Python, PyTorch, OpenCLIP (ViT-B/32), Prototypical Networks, scikit-learn, CUB-200, mini-ImageNet
    Code: GitHub
  • Project Title: When Examples Mislead: Few-Shot Prompting and Object Hallucination in Small VLMs (2026)

    Description:
    Tested whether 0, 1, 3 or 5 in-context examples reduce object hallucination in two small vision-language models (Qwen2-VL-2B and SmolVLM, 4-bit) on 900 balanced POPE questions, scoring yes/no token probabilities and reporting F1, hallucination rate, AUROC, bootstrap 95% confidence intervals and McNemar tests. The examples made the models worse: for Qwen2-VL-2B, F1 fell from 0.832 to 0.761 and the hallucination rate rose from 4.0% to 26.2% at 5-shot (significant on the popular and adversarial splits), while SmolVLM became over-cautious (recall 0.64 to 0.53).
    Tools: Python, PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers, Qwen2-VL-2B, SmolVLM, 4-bit Quantization, POPE
    Code: GitHub
  • Project Title: Not Every Pixel Votes Equally: Clustered Attention Pooling for Limited-Data Recognition (2026)

    Description:
    Replaced global average pooling in a ResNet with attention and clustered attention pooling on CIFAR-100. With 10% of the data, attention raised accuracy (40.47% vs 39.16%) but hurt calibration (ECE 3.45% to 7.67%); with the full data all models reached about 74%. Analysed the models with Grad-CAM.
    Tools: Python, PyTorch, ResNet, CIFAR-100, Grad-CAM, Calibration (ECE)
    Code: GitHub
  • Project Title: Multimodal Fake News Representation Learning with a Variational Autoencoder (2026)
    Mentor: Prof. Dr. Muhammad Affan Alim, Head of Department (Computer Science), IQRA University

    Description:
    Fused Sentence-BERT text features and MobileNetV2 image features for 22,392 FakeNewsNet articles (PolitiFact and GossipCop), trained a VAE to compress them into a 32-dimensional latent space, explored it with PCA, t-SNE and k-means, and compared four classifiers (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, SVM, XGBoost).
    Tools: Python, TensorFlow/Keras, Sentence-BERT, MobileNetV2, scikit-learn, XGBoost, t-SNE
    Code: GitHub
  • Project Title: LungCare: Chest X-Ray Lung Damage Assessment System (BS Final Year Project) (2021)
    Mentor: Miss Maryam Feroz, Lecturer, Department of Computer Science, University of Karachi

    Description:
    Built a web app (React, Flask, Firebase) that takes a chest X-ray and reports how badly the lungs are damaged, using real X-rays of COVID-19, tuberculosis and pneumonia patients from a hospital. Designed the image analysis (lung-area masking and pixel-intensity analysis) with a medical doctor, who validated the results. Presented the project at the codeBIT FYP Symposium, University of Karachi (March 2022).
    Tools: Python, OpenCV, Flask, React, Firebase
    Code: GitHub

Explainable AI

  • Project Title: Systematic Review: Explainable Machine Learning for Distributed Big Data Systems (Jan - Jun 2026)
    Mentor: Dr. Muhammad Umer Farooq, Department of Computer Science and Information Technology, NED University of Engineering and Technology

    Description:
    Reviewed 32 peer-reviewed studies (2020 to 2025) on explainable machine learning in distributed big-data systems using the PRISMA method and six quality criteria (QA1 to QA6), and built a taxonomy linking ML models, XAI techniques and platforms (Apache Spark, Hadoop, Kubernetes, federated learning). Found that SHAP was the most used XAI method (19 of 32 studies) while only 6 of 32 studies measured explanation quality (fidelity, stability, coherence), a key research gap.
    Tools: PRISMA, Systematic Literature Review, Explainable AI (SHAP, LIME), LaTeX

Professional Projects

    Organization: The Disrupt Labs, Karachi
